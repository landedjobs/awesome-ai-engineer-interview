> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Role and evidence.** Most of this page rests on **one anonymous first-person account** for **Software Engineer, Agent** (updated April 2026), which is the role closest to an AI engineer at Sierra. A second account for **Agent Engineer** is older (about 2024) and describes a different sequence. Adjacent roles (Staff Software Engineer, APX) are noted separately. Sierra has not published its round list. Treat everything below as what particular candidates say happened, not as a fixed loop.

> [!NOTE]
> **The loop at a glance (one account).** A **30-minute recruiter call** → a **progressive API coding screen** on CoderPad → an **agent debugging round** (about 4-5 files, about 3 bugs, a diagram as the spec) → an **agent take-home plus a 60-minute presentation** → a **60-minute hiring-manager round** on agency and collaboration. That candidate put the whole process at **two to three weeks**. The distinguishing round is the take-home defense: you build an agent from a supplied API key and prompt, then field "why" questions about architecture and tool calling.

## The loop

What Sierra says officially ([careers page](https://sierra.ai/careers), official, checked Nov 2026): interviews assess your capabilities and how well you align with its values, and they take place at one of its offices because the company works in person.

What one candidate reports ([gaijineer.co account](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience), first-person, anonymous, Software Engineer, Agent, updated 9 Apr 2026, part of a series on interviews in Japan and Singapore):

1. **Recruiter call (30 min)**: background, why Sierra, what you want. No technical questions. You pick Python or TypeScript for the interviews.
2. **Technical screen (CoderPad)**: a practical multi-part problem where each follow-up adds requirements on top of your existing code. Duration not stated.
3. **Debugging round**: a small agent codebase (about 4-5 files) plus a diagram of intended behavior. You can run the agent in the CoderPad terminal. About three bugs, then optimization if time remains. Duration not stated.
4. **Agent building, take-home + 60-minute presentation**: build an agent from an API key and a prompt, then present it to an interviewer who asks many "why" questions. Expected take-home hours are not publicly confirmed.
5. **Hiring-manager round (60 min)**: behavioral, on ownership, driving projects, collaboration, disagreement, and how the role fits your goals.

Outcome: not stated. AI-assistant policy in any round: **not publicly confirmed**.

A different, older account ([Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc), first-person, anonymous, US, interview about two years before Nov 2026, posted about Apr 2026, rejected, six weeks): referral → recruiter screen → one LeetCode-style technical screen → an onsite of a take-home plus three 1-hour rounds. The take-home was to build a small customer support agent and think about prioritization, metrics and observability. The candidate flagged a TypeScript and React debugging round as the hardest part, despite telling the recruiter they mainly used Python. Do not merge this with the 2026 account above; it is a different candidate, role title and period.

**Adjacent roles (not the agent-engineer loop):**

- [Staff Software Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-staff-software-engineer-interview-2e6b7a) (first-person, anonymous, US, interview about Jun 2026, outcome pending): recruiter screen with technical questions, one combined online assessment, then a **75-minute** system design round split into design, execution planning, and coding one component.
- [APX, early-career rotational role](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-interview-67fe22) (first-person, anonymous, US, interview about late 2025, outcome pending, three weeks): a 30-minute recruiter chat, a 1-hour live technical round with one debugging and one Python traversal problem, then an in-person onsite with a product-heavy debugging round and a product design round. The candidate says debugging questions turned into customer-impact questions.

### 🔮 Representative follow-ups you should expect

*Not reported. These are natural next probes given the rounds above.*

- After the flaky API: *"Your retry wraps a call that charges a card. What changes?"* (idempotency keys, retry only safe operations)
- After fallback-ID resolution: *"Two products point at each other's fallback IDs. What does your code do?"* (cycle detection, a visited set, a bounded walk)
- In the debugging round: *"Is this a bug in the code or a gap in the diagram? How do you tell?"*
- In the take-home defense: *"Where would this agent fail on a real customer conversation, and how would you know before the customer did?"*
- In the take-home defense: *"Why not a fixed workflow here instead of letting the model choose tools?"*

## What they're really testing

- **Code that survives changing requirements.** The progressive screen rewards a clean first version that can take the next follow-up without a rewrite.
- **Reading an agent you did not write.** The debugging round is about mapping a diagram to states, tool calls and transitions, then fixing the cause, not the symptom.
- **Owning design decisions after building.** The take-home is only half the round. The 60-minute defense checks whether you can explain why you chose your architecture and tool-calling approach, and what you rejected.
- **Customer-facing judgment.** Sierra's posting describes agent engineers working directly with customers from pilot to production ([Software Engineer, Agent posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Sierra/e9f5fdb6-91ee-4c55-9230-41ec8865650e), official). The APX account says even debugging turned into customer-impact questions.
- **Agency and collaboration.** The hiring-manager round digs into specific examples of ownership and disagreement.

## How to prep

- **Agent loops, tool schemas, stopping rules** → [content/03](../content/03-agents-and-tool-use.md). Know when a fixed workflow beats a model-directed agent; Anthropic's [Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents) (📰 article) is a good frame for the "why this architecture?" question.
- **Take-home and presentation** → [content/11](../content/11-take-homes-and-work-trials.md) for how to scope a take-home, keep a decision log, and prepare the defense. Build one agent end to end first with the [portfolio project guides](https://github.com/landedjobs/ai-engineer-portfolio-projects/tree/main/guides) (💻 repo).
- **Evals and observability for your agent** → [content/04](../content/04-evals-and-llm-as-judge.md) and [answers/eval-pipeline.md](../answers/eval-pipeline.md). Bring a small eval set and a failure log to the presentation.
- **Customer-support agent design** → [answers/support-bot.md](../answers/support-bot.md) and [answers/agentic-workflow.md](../answers/agentic-workflow.md).
- **Progressive API coding and debugging** → the coding and agents tiers in [questions/coding.md](../questions/coding.md) and [questions/agents.md](../questions/agents.md). Practice retries with backoff, timeouts and graph traversal with cycle checks in Python or TypeScript.
- **AI-assistant policy** → not publicly confirmed for any Sierra round. Ask your recruiter, and prepare both ways with [content/10](../content/10-ai-assisted-coding-round.md).
- **Hiring-manager round** → [questions/behavioral.md](../questions/behavioral.md). Prepare two concrete ownership stories and one disagreement story with details you can defend.

## Culture signals

Sierra's postings list five values: **Trust, Customer Obsession, Craftsmanship, Intensity, and Family** ([Software Engineer, Agent posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Sierra/e9f5fdb6-91ee-4c55-9230-41ec8865650e), official). The careers page says interviews assess values fit alongside skill, and that interviews happen in person at an office ([careers page](https://sierra.ai/careers), official). Come ready to talk about customers and outcomes, not only code.
